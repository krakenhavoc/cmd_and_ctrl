## Context

The pinned Comprehensive Rules for this tree (effective **August 7, 2026**,
AGENTS.md §6) file gift under **CR 702.174**. Issue #1267 cites 702.170 from an
older edition; every citation here and in the code is 702.174, checked against the
pinned text:

> **702.174a** Gift is a keyword that represents two abilities. It is written "Gift a
> [something]." The first ability is a static ability that functions while the card
> with gift is on the stack, and the second is either an ability that functions while
> the card with gift is on the stack or a triggered ability that functions while the
> card with gift is on the battlefield. The first ability is always "As an additional
> cost to cast this spell, you may choose an opponent." Paying a spell's gift cost
> follows the rules for paying additional costs in rules 601.2b and 601.2f–h. …
>
> **702.174b** On a permanent, the second ability represented by gift is "When this
> permanent enters, if its gift cost was paid, [effect]." On an instant or sorcery
> spell, the second ability represented by gift is "If this spell's gift cost was
> paid, [effect]." …
>
> **702.174d–i** "Gift a Food" … "The chosen player creates a Food token." "Gift a card"
> … "draws a card." "Gift a tapped Fish" … "creates a tapped 1/1 blue Fish creature
> token." "Gift an extra turn" … "Gift a Treasure" … "Gift an Octopus" …
>
> **702.174j** For instant and sorcery spells, the effect of a gift ability always
> happens before any other spell abilities of the card. If the spell is countered or
> otherwise leaves the stack before resolving, the gift effect doesn't happen.
>
> **702.174k** If a spell's controller declares the intention to pay a spell's gift
> cost, that spell's gift was promised.
>
> **702.174m** If part of a spell's ability has its effect only if its gift was
> promised, and that part of the ability includes any targets, the spell's controller
> chooses those targets only if the gift was promised.

Six catalog cards shipped weaker than printed because nothing could express this —
Dawn's Truce, Into the Flood Maw, Long River's Pull, Peerless Recycling, Starfall
Invocation and Wear Down, each with a caveat saying the gift can't be promised.

Three facts in the rules text decide the design:

1. **It is a cost** (702.174a), announced at CR 601.2b and paid at 601.2f–h, optional.
   The engine has exactly one optional-cost machine (ADR 0073), and it already does
   every step a gift needs: an index in the announcement, validation before the
   targets, a record on the stack item, CR 707.10 copying, CR 400.7d carrying onto
   the permanent, snapshot and undo.
2. **Paying it names a player**, which no existing cost component does. Kicker,
   buyback and offspring pay mana or cards.
3. **The promise can change the target clause** (702.174m, and every "instead …
   target …" gift card). The promise is made at 601.2b and the targets at 601.2c, so
   the clause the targets are judged against is the one the announcement produced —
   the same ordering argument ADR 0073 §2 made for a kicked spell, and the same shape
   cleave has for an alternative cost (`AlternativeCost.Targets`).

## Decisions

### 1. The cost is an `AdditionalCost` with `ChoosesOpponent`, keyed `"gift"`

`game.AdditionalCost` gains `ChoosesOpponent bool`. A gift cost is
`{Optional: true, Key: game.GiftKey, ChoosesOpponent: true, Label: "Gift a card"}` in
the card's optional-cost index space. The announcement is the index in
`CastSpellParams.OptionalCosts` **plus** `CastSpellParams.GiftOpponent`, the chosen
player.

`validateGiftChoiceLocked` runs in `CastSpell` right after
`validateOptionalCostChoice`, before targets: a gift index requires a recipient, the
recipient must be in `GiftOpponentsLocked(caster)` — every other seat not
`Eliminated` — and a recipient sent with no gift index is **refused, not ignored**. A
client that named a recipient thought it was promising a gift, and silently casting
the ungifted spell is the worst available failure (ADR 0073's reasoning about an
unoffered optional cost, one field over).

Rejected: a new `GiftCost` type or a `PendingChoice` for the opponent. The first
would grow its own copy of index, record, copy and carry; the second has no frame
for a half-validated cast (the argument `CastSpellParams.Face` already makes). Every
other CR 601.2b decision is an announce-time parameter.

### 2. The promise is a paid-cost fact: `PaidCost.GiftOpponent`, then `CastProvenance.GiftOpponent`

`PaidCost.GiftOpponent uuid.UUID` holds the chosen player; non-nil **is** CR
702.174k's "the gift was promised" (`PaidCost.GiftPromised()`). It sits beside
`OptionalCosts` for the same reason: by resolution nothing on the board records the
choice.

- **CR 707.10:** a copy of a promised spell is promised to the same opponent —
  `createSpellCopyLocked` copies it, and the permanent-spell copy's token template
  carries it on its provenance.
- **CR 400.7d:** `stampCastProvenanceLocked` copies it to
  `CastProvenance.GiftOpponent`, so a permanent's "when this enters, if the gift was
  promised" reads `card.GiftPromised()`. It clears with the rest of the provenance on
  every zone change (CR 400.7), so a reanimated Scrapshooter was not promised.
- **Snapshot / undo:** both structs are value-serialised already; the field rides
  along, and the round trip is pinned.
- **The wire:** `StackItemView.gift_to` — public, because who was promised a gift and
  whether a Long River's Pull counters any spell are both things a responder needs.

### 3. A paid optional cost may rewrite the target clause: `AdditionalCost.Targets`

`AdditionalCost.Targets *TargetSpec`, when non-nil, **replaces** the spell's clause
when that optional cost is paid. `game.TargetSpecUnderOptionalCosts(base, optional,
paid)` is applied after `TargetSpecUnderAlternativeCost` at every place the latter is
applied: announce (`CastSpell`), resolution and copies (`castTargetSpecForItem`), the
bot enumerator (`legal/cast.go`) and the view (`OptionalCostView.target_mode /
legal_targets / clauses`). One function, the same order, so the three never disagree.

**The snapshot restore was the fourth place, and it had a bug of its own.** A
restored spell re-derived its `targetSpec` from `CatalogTargetSpec` — the PRINTED
clause — so a cleaved Wash Away restored mid-stack was judged at resolution under
its un-cleaved clause, and a promised Long River's Pull would have fizzled against
its Bolt. `restoreStackItem` now calls `castTargetSpecForItem`, the one function
that applies both rewrites (`TestGiftPromisedClauseSurvivesARestore`).

It is a field on the cost rather than on gift because nothing about it is gift-shaped
— "if kicked, instead target …" is the same rewrite — but gift is the only declarer
today.

A clause only the promise has (Valley Rally's "if the gift was promised, target
creature you control gains first strike", CR 702.174m) is a rewrite from nothing to
something. That card has no printed clause, so `CastSpell` would have fallen back to
the S13.1 free-form behaviour and accepted any target on an unpromised cast; a card
whose optional costs declare a clause is refused a target on a cast that does not pay
one (`optionalCostsDeclareTargets`).

Precedence, written down so it is a decision: alternative cost first, then the first
paid optional cost that carries a clause. No printed card has both. `Register` panics
on a rewrite on a modal card, whose clauses live on its modes.

### 4. The card declares gift once: `Spec.Gift`, grown by `buildDef`

```go
Gift: GiftACard(),                                                   // Dawn's Truce
Gift: GiftACard().Instead(TargetSpell("target spell")),              // Long River's Pull
Gift: GiftATappedFish().Instead(TargetPermanent(…, Nonland(), …)),   // Into the Flood Maw
Gift: GiftAFood().Instead(TargetCreature("target creature you control", YouControl())), // Valley Rally
```

The constructors (`GiftACard`, `GiftAFood`, `GiftATappedFish`, `GiftATreasure`) tie the
printed noun to the CR 702.174d–h effect. From the one field `buildDef` grows three
things, as it does for madness and suspend:

- **the cost**, appended **last** to the card's optional costs so a printed kicker
  keeps its index;
- **the spell half** (CR 702.174b, j): the catalog resolve hook is wrapped so a
  promised instant or sorcery gives the gift **before** `OnResolve`. A countered spell
  never reaches the hook and a fizzled one (CR 608.2b) never runs it, which is
  702.174j's last sentence for free;
- **the permanent half** (CR 702.174b): an ETB trigger with a CR 603.4 intervening if
  on `source.GiftPromised()`. The recipient is read in `Build` and captured as a value,
  because the permanent may be gone by the time the trigger resolves.

Both halves are installed on every gift card, and neither needs the card's type: the
wrapper stands aside for a permanent spell, and the trigger never fires for an
instant because an instant never enters. `Register` refuses a hand-rolled gift cost
in `OptionalCosts` (it would have no gift behind it) and a `Gift` built outside the
constructors (it would have no effect).

The card's own "if the gift was promised" branches read `ctx.GiftPromised()` in
`OnResolve`, and a permanent's own trigger reads `source.GiftPromised()` — Scrapshooter
is both halves at once.

**A recipient who left the game** (CR 800.4a) receives nothing, but the gift **was
still promised** (702.174k is about the declaration) and the card's promised branch
still applies. Pinned by a test.

Token gifts come from the token catalog (ADR 0083): the Food and the Treasure are the
catalog templates with their abilities, and the Fish is `TokenCard("1/1 blue Fish")`
through `TokenSpec.EntersTapped()`, so it enters tapped rather than being tapped a
beat later.

### 5. The bot is offered each opponent

`legal.castMovesForCard` expands every announced optional-cost set that includes a
gift into one announcement per entry of `GiftOpponentsLocked` — the same list the
engine validates against and the view offers — and prices, gates and targets each
under the promised clause. The unpromised cast is always offered too. A bot that
could only promise to the first seat could never choose who to feed; one that was
never offered the promise could never take Long River's Pull's wider line. Every
enumerated move is dispatched in the tests (#544's agreement).

### 6. The client asks in the prompt that already asks

The optional-cost toggles in `AlternativeCostModal` are where CR 601.2b is asked. A
gift toggle opens a radio list of `opponent_options` by name, Cast is disabled until
one is picked, and the chosen opponent rides `cast_spell` as `gift_opponent`. The
targeting walk takes its clause from `castTargetOverride` — the alternative cost's,
else a claimed optional cost's, else the card's — so Valley Rally opens a picker only
when promised. The stack shows "Gift → name".

## Out of scope

- **Gift an extra turn** (Perch Protection) and **Gift an Octopus** (Octomancer):
  there is no extra-turn primitive, and no card waiting on the Octopus justifies a token
  row today. Each is one constructor the day it is needed.
- **"Whenever a player gives a gift"** (CR 702.174c): no catalog card reads it. It
  would be an event emitted from the two gift halves.
- **Starfall Invocation** keeps its caveat: its promised branch needs a pick among the
  creature cards *this* wipe put into your graveyard. Offering the promise without it
  would hand the opponent a card for nothing, so it stays unpromisable and its caveat
  is pinned in `coverage/caveats_test.go` under the new `gift` probe.
- **Choosing two different optional costs at once** is still not enumerated for bots
  (ADR 0073 §9); no gift card also prints a kicker.
