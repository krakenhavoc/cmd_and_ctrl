**Protection (CR 702.16)** (#662, [ADR 0072](decisions/0072-protection.md), superseding [ADR 0038 §7](decisions/0038-protection-style-keywords.md)) — closes the protection row (count **38**). All four DEBT checks are against the **source object**, never its controller: targeting (CR 702.16b), attachment (CR 702.16c-d), damage (CR 702.16e) and blocking (CR 702.16f). The quality is a parameterised token in `Characteristic.Abilities` ("protection from red", "protection from Demons") with ONE closed-grammar reader, `game/protection.go` — colours, card types, creature subtypes and "everything"; a quality it cannot parse mints no token and leaves the card flagged by the ADR 0037 signal, which is how "protection from monocolored" still reads as unimplemented. The targeting refactor is the body of the work: `game.TargetSource{Controller, Object, Snapshot}` replaced the bare caster ID at `targets.go`'s choke point and every targeting call site now names its source — cast, activation, trigger pick, pending-choice resume, the spell-copy re-target (a value copy, because the original can be countered), the CR 608.2b re-check, the view's legal-target stamps and `internal/legal`'s `legalTargetSets`. Damage reads `ReplacementEvent.SourceLKI`, a `Characteristic` snapshot taken where the event is created, so a SPELL (never on the battlefield) and an attacker already swept into a graveyard both answer correctly. Prevention is one engine built-in beside #960's regeneration shield, declared `Preemptive` so it applies before every other replacement and spends no prevention-shield charge (#420) — a declared simplification of CR 616.1. Shipped on **5 cards**: **Baneslayer Angel** (a subtype quality; a changeling counts as a Demon through #939's `AllCreatureTypes`), **Sword of Fire and Ice**, **Sword of Feast and Famine**, **Animar, Soul of Elements** — all four clearing their protection caveats — and **Mother of Runes**, new, on #742's existing `choose_color` kind. **Protection FROM a player (CR 702.16k) closed with #980** — see the row below. Player protection — a PLAYER having it — closed with #1197 (its own entry above). Still open: CR 615.12 "can't be prevented" (modelled nowhere), and CR 616.1 ordering against protection.
