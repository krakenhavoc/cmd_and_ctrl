**"Can't block alone", and tokens printed with attack and block limits** (#5396; CR 506.5, 509.1b, 802.4b) — "can't attack alone" was an attack limit (`game.AttackLimitNotAlone`, Pet Sehlat) but nothing said a creature can't block alone, and a token template could carry a block rule but no attack limit. `game.BlockRule.NotAlone` (`effects.CantBlockAlone`, `game/block_not_alone.go`) is a restriction on the rule's own source, judged on the whole declaration: the declaration verbs refuse one that leaves it as its controller's only blocker with the new reason `cant_block_alone` (another player's blockers are no company, CR 802.4b), and a declaration that completes with it alone anyway — its companion taken back by `remove_from_combat` or gone before the defender finishes — takes it out before anything is announced or taxed. Two such creatures block together. The option generator offers the creature with one companion on the same attacker while nothing is staged, and its singles once a companion is; the view's `block_refusals` carries the reason, the card's `cant_block_alone` stamp lets the bot's block-set search (`blockset.go`) refuse a set it would be alone in, and `protocol.ts` mirrors both. `tokenTemplate.AttackLimits` registers a token's printed attack limits beside its `BlockRules`. Plain data, so no closure route and no snapshot change. **Card now Full:** Toby, Beastie Befriender, whose 4/4 white Beast is printed with both.
