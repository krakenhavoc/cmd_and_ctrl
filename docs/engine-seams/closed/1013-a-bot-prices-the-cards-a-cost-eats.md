**A bot prices the cards a cost eats** (#1013, [ADR 0033 §1 amendment](decisions/0033-ai-bot-seat.md)): the enumerator offered exactly ONE payment for the card-shaped half of an alternative cost (`legal.maxEnumeratedCostPayments` was 1) and built it out of the first N candidates in ZONE order, so an escaping Uro over a graveyard holding a second Uro, a Snapcaster target and three lands ate whichever three were oldest. The cap was never the constraint — the missing EVALUATION was, because a card in a graveyard was worth nothing to `aiseat/heuristic` and two payments were indistinguishable to it. **`aiseat.CostFuelPricer`** is the price: `TargetOrderer`’s twin, asked once per decision and threaded in as `legal.Options.OrderCostFuel`; a second hook rather than a second meaning for the first, because the two ask OPPOSITE questions (a target order ranks the board by importance and the enumerator keeps the TOP; a fuel price ranks your own cards by what you would lose and it spends the BOTTOM). The heuristic’s answer is one function, `heuristic.fuelValue` (`aiseat/heuristic/fuel.go`), read BOTH by the enumerator (which payment to offer) and by `valueOfCast` (what the payment it was offered costs) — a second scorer would drift and the bot would take a payment it then priced as a mistake. It closes #673’s declared gap on the way: a non-hand cast still costs no card in HAND, and what it really spends is now priced. The cap rises to THREE and ADR 0033 §1’s corollary still holds, because the extra payments come out of whatever `MaxExpansionPerSource` the TARGET walk did not use — an Uro with no targets gets its alternatives, a removal spell over a wide board gets none. Still open and named in the amendment: the price does not know what the spell being cast IS, and cannot see a graveyard synergy the wire does not carry (a Crucible makes a land in the graveyard worth keeping, and `FuelFloor` does not know).
