**"Has convoke" as a readable property, and a next-spell promise of convoke** (#5647, CR 702.51a / 702.51d / 601.2i) — a next-spell promise could give improvise but not convoke, and nothing could tell a cast trigger that the spell being cast has convoke. Both are now data. `game.NextSpellPromise.Convoke` is improvise's twin: while the promise stands, `TapPermanentsCostForCastLocked` offers the caster's next matching spell `game.ConvokeTapCost`, so validation, pricing and the cast view agree, and that spell spends the promise whether or not a creature was tapped. `effects.NextSpellHasConvoke(from)` is the card-side step.
- **The read.** `StackItem.HadConvoke` is stamped by the cast, with every cost paid and just before the promises are spent (the promise is gone by the time `EventCast` is out). It is true when the cast's tap cost has a convoke link: printed (`Spec.TapCost`), given by a permanent (`GrantedTapCosts`, read with the zone the spell is cast from) or given by a promise. `Game.SpellHadConvokeForEffect` reads it and `effects.YouCastASpellThatHasConvoke` is the trigger condition. Whether a creature was tapped is the older, separate `Paid.Convoked`. A copy is not cast (CR 707.10), so it carries no stamp.
- **Chained, not first-wins.** `TapPermanentsCostForCastLocked` used to return the card's own tap cost and look no further. It now chains the card's own cost, the promised one and every granted one through `TapPermanentsCost.WithAlso` (#4177's chain), so a spell with improvise of its own that is promised or granted convoke has both and may tap artifacts and creatures. A second instance of one keyword still adds nothing (CR 702.51d). `TapPermanentsCost.HasConvoke` asks a cost, chain included.
- **Snapshot.** Additive within the schema version: `hadConvoke` on a stack item and `convoke` on a next-spell promise. No wire change: the cast view already reads the tap cost through the one function.

**Cards:** Wand of the Worldsoul, Flockchaser Phantom, Joyful Stormsculptor, Kasla, the Broken Halo, Saint Traft and Rem Karolus, Invasion of Segovia // Caetus, Sea Tyrant of Segovia.
